The interesting part is rarely the alert. It’s finding the pressure point underneath it.

Memory pressureHM / 01

The service kept running out of memory at peak.

pprof exposed the OOM path; New Relic traces pointed to N+1 and unbounded database queries.

I coordinated fixes across the query paths and teams. The related production incidents stopped recurring.

pprof · New Relic · Go

Lock contentionHM / 02

Incoming messages arrived up to ten minutes late.

Frequent writes to conversation rows—sometimes across 10,000 conversations—were making PostgreSQL wait on itself.

Using pg_stat_activity and a blocking-process map, I traced the contention and redesigned unread-state storage. Peak-hour blocking effectively disappeared; the PagerDuty alert stopped recurring.

PostgreSQL · concurrency · incident response

Lost state on deployHM / 03

A million-recipient broadcast shouldn’t depend on one process staying alive.

The previous in-memory design could lose its place during a deployment.

I led a rebuild around durable Temporal workflows with configurable retries, plus timezone-aware delivery. Progress survived deployments and broadcasts could reach 1M+ recipients.

Temporal · durable workflows · messaging

At the scene: New Relic follows the trace, pprof checks memory, and Delve asks the goroutine where it was at the time of the race.
